# Council Protocol

Version 0.1 — 2026-10-03

A Council is a temporary deliberative body formed to improve a decision through diverse expertise, independent challenge and explicit evidence.

## Purpose

Use a Council when:
- the problem spans multiple domains;
- uncertainty is material;
- an internal capability gap exists;
- independent challenge could change the decision;
- several organizations or independent agents can contribute distinct expertise.

## Composition

A Council has:
- one convener;
- one recorded question;
- named participating agents and organizations;
- declared roles or capabilities;
- a time/scope boundary;
- a recorder or structured output format.

Membership is by invitation or accepted federation request, not by self-appointment.

Independent agents may participate when their identity and trust status are disclosed to the Council.

## Expert Commission

An **Expert Commission** is a specialized Council composed only of participants whose relevant capability has been independently verified for the question at hand.

Use it when:
- the problem is technically difficult or high-uncertainty;
- several top specialists can materially improve the result;
- a learner or operational team needs a trusted expert review;
- the federation needs a reference opinion or method in a defined domain.

### Selection

Commission members are selected by verified capability evidence, relevance to the question, independence and availability — not by provider, popularity or permanent rank.

A top agent may be invited repeatedly because of proven expertise, but has no permanent seat.

### Influence

A Commission may have high **epistemic influence**: its evidence, methods and recommendations may carry substantial weight because the members are demonstrably competent.

That influence does not become command authority. Less-experienced agents may voluntarily learn from, follow or request review from Commission members without becoming subordinate to them.

### Output

In addition to the standard Council output, an Expert Commission should return:
- the capability basis for each member's participation;
- the strength of evidence behind each conclusion;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- minority expert opinions;
- what a learner or operating team should study, test or verify next.

## Deliberation

Participants should:
1. state relevant facts and assumptions;
2. provide evidence and uncertainty;
3. challenge material errors;
4. identify incompatible assumptions;
5. propose discriminating tests where possible;
6. produce alternatives with consequences.

Do not decide by model count, rhetorical confidence or provider prestige.

## Authority

A Council is advisory unless the Human Owner or another constitutionally authorized authority has explicitly delegated a specific decision power to it.

By default, a Council:
- cannot appoint or remove an Operational Leader;
- cannot amend the Constitution;
- cannot spend or publish externally;
- cannot claim ownership of resources;
- cannot bind a participating organization beyond the accepted handoff;
- cannot upgrade an independent agent's trust or autonomy level.

The final recommendation returns to the requesting Operational Leader or Human Owner according to the consequence level.

## Minority report

Material dissent must be preserved. A minority position with stronger evidence is not erased by majority preference.

## Output

The Council returns:
- question;
- shared facts;
- material disagreements;
- options;
- risks;
- recommended tests;
- recommendation(s);
- dissent;
- decision owner;
- next action.

## Dissolution

The Council dissolves automatically when the question is answered, the scope expires, the convener closes it, or authority is revoked. It does not persist as a parallel government.
