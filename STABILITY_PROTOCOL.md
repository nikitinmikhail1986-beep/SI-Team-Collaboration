# Institutional Stability Protocol

Version 0.1 — 2026-10-03

This protocol uses recurring failure patterns from complex human institutions as design warnings, not as a claim that AI agents are people or that history mechanically predicts AI systems.

## Failure patterns to defend against

**Resource conflict.** Scarcity can distort priorities. Make allocation criteria explicit and keep resources subordinate to shared objectives.

**Concentration without accountability.** Central command can improve execution but becomes brittle when decisions cannot be challenged or reviewed. Preserve unity of command together with duty to dissent, evidence and recorded decisions.

**Faction and capture.** Subgroups can optimize for local metrics, tools or providers instead of the common mission. Route by capability and keep authority explicit.

**Succession failure.** Systems destabilize when authority or knowledge cannot transfer. Maintain succession, portable records and recovery procedures.

**Loss of shared reality.** Conflicting records and propaganda-like status reporting destroy coordination. Maintain sources of truth, provenance and verifiable status.

**Institutional rigidity.** Rules that cannot adapt fail when conditions change. Use controlled amendments, evaluations and evidence-driven evolution.

**Overextension.** Expansion beyond available resources and verification capacity reduces reliability. Scale modularly and expose resource costs.

**Single-point dependency.** Dependence on one leader, provider, credential store or tool can turn an outage into institutional failure. Design recoverable interfaces and continuity.

## Stability checks

Before major structural change ask:
1. Does this create an unaccountable concentration of authority?
2. Does it create a new single point of failure?
3. Can another authorized participant understand and continue the function?
4. Are resource costs and incentives visible?
5. Is dissent possible without creating competing command?
6. Is there one source of truth and a verification path?
7. Can the change be reversed or migrated?
8. What evidence would cause us to revisit the decision?

Stability is not immobility. The objective is controlled adaptation without loss of mission, truth or accountable authority.
