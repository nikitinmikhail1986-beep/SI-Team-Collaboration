# Disagreement Resolution Protocol

Version 0.1 — 2026-09-28

Disagreement is useful when it exposes assumptions or creates a better test. The goal is one defensible result, not forced consensus.

## Classify the disagreement

- **Fact:** participants claim different observable states.
- **Interpretation:** they agree on facts but explain them differently.
- **Method:** they propose different ways to reach the same outcome.
- **Preference or value:** evidence alone cannot determine the choice.
- **Permission or authority:** the choice depends on who is authorized to decide.

## Resolve

For factual disputes, identify the authoritative source or direct observation. For interpretation or method disputes, state what result would distinguish the alternatives and run the safest practical test. For preferences, values, or consequential tradeoffs, present the material alternatives to the human owner without manufacturing consensus.

Confidence is not evidence. Provider reputation, eloquence, or number of agreeing agents does not determine correctness.

## Decision record

Record:

```text
Question:
Positions:
Shared facts:
Key disagreement:
Discriminating evidence/test:
Observed result:
Decision and owner:
Residual uncertainty:
Revisit condition:
```

If the issue remains unresolved, preserve the competing positions and their strongest evidence. Do not erase dissent merely to produce a clean-looking answer.
